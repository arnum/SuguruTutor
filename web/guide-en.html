The order in practice

  1. Go through the whole grid for naked and hidden singles until nothing more happens.
  2. Look for regions of two cells and for 2×2 blocks. That is where neighbour eliminations and triples usually hide.
  3. Pencil in the possible numbers and look for pairs that see each other.
  4. Find cells with two options and numbers with two places in a region, and see whether they can be chained. Start with one number at a time.
  5. Only when nothing else works: assume a number and follow it. Start with just that one number, then all numbers, and finally try both ways.

In the game, a puzzle's difficulty is measured as effort: the heaviest step plus an addition for how many heavy steps there are. The level shown for each technique below is its place from easy to hard, not the difficulty of a puzzle. Examples marked “From a real puzzle” come straight from the generator, and every claim has been checked against the puzzles' solutions.
